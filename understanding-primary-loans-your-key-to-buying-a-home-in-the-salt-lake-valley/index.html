Understanding Primary Loans: Your Key to Buying a Home in the Salt Lake Valley

FinancingFirst-Time BuyersInvesting

If you are buying your first house along the Wasatch Front, the most consequential decision after the house itself is which primary residence loan in Utah you take. The same borrower can qualify for an FHA loan, a conventional loan with 3% down, a Utah Housing Corporation (UHC) FirstHome, and (for veterans) a VA loan simultaneously — and the right answer among them depends on your credit score, down-payment cash, plan to refinance, and the specific Salt Lake, Utah, Davis, Weber, Summit, Wasatch, Tooele, or Cache county home you are buying. This guide compares the four primary residence loan products Utah first-time buyers actually use.

This article is the loan-product comparison; it is not the rate-shopping guide and not the DTI math. For the lender shop, see our Salt Lake City mortgage rate guide; for the qualifying math, see how to afford a house in the Salt Lake Valley.

FHA — the 3.5% down, flexible-credit path

FHA (Federal Housing Administration) loans are the default primary residence loan Utah first-time buyers reach for when credit is below the conventional-friendly 700-720 FICO range. Key terms in 2026:

  • Minimum down payment: 3.5% with a 580+ FICO; 10% with 500-579.
  • Debt-to-income (DTI) ceiling: up to 56.9% with compensating factors; most Wasatch Front underwriters stop at 50%.
  • Mortgage insurance: upfront MIP of 1.75% (financed into the loan) plus annual MIP of 0.55% for standard 30-year loans with less than 10% down, paid for the life of the loan.
  • County loan limits: HUD sets FHA limits per county. Salt Lake and Utah counties run in the mid-$500Ks; Summit County is substantially higher. Check the current limit for your target county at the HUD FHA lookup.

FHA is the right call if your credit is in the 580-680 range, if you are close to the DTI ceiling, or if you intend to use Utah Housing Corporation DPA on top (UHC runs a FirstHome FHA product). FHA mortgage insurance is sticky — the only way off it is to refinance into a conventional loan once your LTV is below 80%, so plan to refinance within 3-5 years in most cases.

VA — zero down, no PMI (eligible veterans)

If you qualify, VA is almost always the strongest primary residence loan in Utah. Eligible active-duty, veteran, and surviving-spouse borrowers get:

  • 0% down payment up to the VA entitlement limit.
  • No private mortgage insurance ever — just a one-time VA funding fee (1.25%-3.3% depending on down payment and prior use, financed into the loan and waived for disability-rated veterans).
  • Competitive rates typically 0.25-0.5 points below comparable conventional, and more forgiving DTI/credit underwriting.
  • No county cap for borrowers with full entitlement (the 2020 Blue Water Navy Vietnam Veterans Act removed the county limit for full-entitlement loans).

VA loans in Utah are underwritten against the home's VA appraisal plus Minimum Property Requirements — peeling paint on pre-1978 homes, missing handrails, and unpermitted work can all stall the loan. Have your buyer's agent flag MPR issues before you write the offer.

Conventional — the flexible all-purpose primary residence loan

Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac conventional loans are what most Wasatch Front first-time buyers close on once their credit is 680+ and their down payment is 3-5% or more. Two sub-products to know:

  • HomeReady (Fannie) and Home Possible (Freddie) — 3% down for first-time buyers below the area median income (AMI) cap. Reduced PMI, flexible income sources (including boarder income), and the option to use a non-occupant co-borrower.
  • Standard conventional — 5% down (or more). Full PMI until LTV hits 80%, then it drops off automatically at 78%. No income cap.

Conventional loans conform to the Federal Housing Finance Agency loan limit. In 2026 the one-unit conforming baseline runs in the low-$800Ks for most Utah counties, with Summit County sitting at a materially higher high-cost limit. Any loan amount above those caps is a jumbo, which has its own underwriting and pricing.

The best feature of conventional for a primary residence in Utah is that PMI goes away. Unlike FHA mortgage insurance, conventional PMI drops off automatically once your LTV reaches 78% (or you can request manual cancellation at 80% with an appraisal). Over a 10-year hold, that difference is five figures.

UHC — Utah Housing Corporation first mortgages with DPA built in

Utah Housing Corporation is the state housing finance agency. Its first-mortgage products — FirstHome, HomeAgain, Score 580, and No MI — wrap around an FHA, VA, USDA-RD, or conventional loan and pair it with a second-mortgage down-payment-assistance loan covering up to 100% of your down payment and closing costs. The first-mortgage rate on a UHC loan is set weekly by UHC, not shopped lender-to-lender.

UHC is the right choice when you need the DPA to close, when your Utah county is inside the UHC income cap, and when you plan to hold the home at least five years (there is a small DPA recapture tax if you sell for a large gain in the first nine years). It is the wrong choice when you have the down payment in cash and a 720+ FICO — at that point a straight conventional loan is cheaper over the life of the loan.

Utah county loan limits (2026)

Two separate caps affect which primary residence loan in Utah you can use at a given price:

  • FHFA conforming limit (conventional): $832,750 baseline for one-unit homes in most Utah counties, materially higher in high-cost Summit County. Above the cap, it's a jumbo loan.
  • HUD FHA limit: varies widely by county. Salt Lake, Utah, Davis, Weber, Tooele, and Cache counties fall in the mid-$500Ks for one-unit; Summit and Wasatch counties are substantially higher. Confirm your target county at HUD's lookup before writing on a home above that number.

If your target price is above the FHA cap but below the conforming limit, conventional (or UHC's conventional product) is your path.

Choosing the right primary residence loan in Utah for your scenario

A quick decision tree:

  • Active-duty, veteran, or eligible surviving spouse: start with VA. It is almost always the best primary residence loan in Utah for eligible buyers.
  • FICO 580-679, low down-payment cash: FHA, likely paired with UHC DPA. FHA accommodates the credit; UHC covers the down payment.
  • FICO 680-719, 3-5% down available, below AMI: Conventional HomeReady or Home Possible. Lower PMI than FHA, PMI drops off automatically, no life-of-loan MI.
  • FICO 720+, 5-20% down: Standard conventional. You have access to the lowest rates and the cleanest exit from PMI.
  • Any scenario, need the DPA: Add UHC on top of the underlying first mortgage product.

Frequently Asked Questions

What type of loan is best for a primary residence in Utah?

For eligible veterans, VA is almost always the best primary residence loan in Utah — 0% down, no PMI, competitive rates. For most first-time buyers under 720 FICO with limited down payment cash, FHA (often paired with Utah Housing Corporation DPA) is the fit. For 720+ FICO buyers with 5-20% down, a conventional loan beats FHA because PMI drops off automatically at 78% LTV.

What is the difference between FHA and conventional loans in Utah?

FHA accepts lower credit scores (580 for 3.5% down), tolerates higher DTI, and carries lower base rates on marginal credit — but mortgage insurance is sticky and typically only removable by refinancing. Conventional requires 620+ FICO (best pricing at 740+), lets PMI drop off automatically at 78% LTV, and has no upfront mortgage insurance premium. FHA is a path in; conventional is a path forward.

Can I get a conventional loan with 3% down in Utah?

Yes, through Fannie Mae's HomeReady or Freddie Mac's Home Possible programs. Both allow 3% down for first-time buyers whose household income is at or below the area median income for the Utah county they are buying in. HomeReady and Home Possible also carry lower PMI rates than standard conventional, so they are often cheaper than FHA for buyers in the 680-720 FICO band.

What are Utah's conforming loan limits in 2026?

The one-unit conforming loan limit for most Utah counties in 2026 sits in the low-$800Ks ($832,750), which is the FHFA baseline for the nation. Summit County carries a materially higher high-cost limit. Loans above the applicable county cap are jumbo and underwrite to different guidelines. Check the current limit for your target county at the FHFA website.

Is a VA loan better than FHA in Utah?

For eligible veterans, yes — almost always. VA requires 0% down versus FHA's 3.5%, has no monthly PMI (just a one-time funding fee, waived for disability-rated veterans), typically prices 0.25-0.5 points below FHA, and is more forgiving on DTI. The only common exception is a property that cannot pass VA Minimum Property Requirements — in that case FHA becomes the fallback.
