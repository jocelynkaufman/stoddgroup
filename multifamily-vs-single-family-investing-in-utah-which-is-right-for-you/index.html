Multifamily vs. Single-Family Investing in Utah: Which Is Right for You?

First-Time Buyers House Hacking Investing

If you are deciding between a Wasatch Front single-family rental and a 2-4 unit or 5+ unit multifamily, the choice is not about one being "better" — it is about which asset class matches your capital, your tolerance for operational complexity, and your target hold period. This guide lays out the multifamily vs single family Utah decision matrix the way we walk it with investor clients in 2026: cash flow, financing, management load, appreciation drivers, and exit strategy, side by side.

This article is intentionally scoped to the SFR-vs-MFR asset-class decision. For the underwriting math itself, see our Utah rental property cash flow and ROI guide; for market-level investor analysis across the state, see our top Utah areas to invest guide.

The multifamily vs single family Utah decision matrix at a glance

Six axes to compare before you commit capital:

  • Cash flow per dollar invested: 2-4 unit multifamily typically beats single-family on a Utah dollar basis; 5+ unit beats 2-4 unit when well-operated.
  • Appreciation profile: single-family appreciation is set by the owner-occupant resale market (comps in the neighborhood). Multifamily (5+ unit) appreciation is set by net operating income (NOI) and cap rates — you can force appreciation by raising rents and cutting expenses.
  • Financing: 1-4 unit (including duplex, triplex, fourplex) qualifies for conventional 30-year financing with a 15-25% down payment if non-owner-occupied. 5+ unit requires commercial or DSCR financing with 25-30% down and 20-25 year amortizations.
  • Management complexity: SFR tenants turn less often and complain less often. MFR tenants have higher turnover and more overlapping issues; 5+ unit usually requires a property-management company.
  • Tenant mix: SFR attracts longer-term family households; MFR attracts younger, more mobile tenants. Both work in Utah; the mix fits different operator profiles.
  • Exit strategy: SFR sells to owner-occupants (deep buyer pool). Multifamily sells to other investors (narrower buyer pool, more time on market).

Cash flow: multifamily usually wins per dollar invested

On a dollar-in basis in 2026 Wasatch Front markets, multifamily cash-flows meaningfully better than single-family. A rough gauge from current deal flow:

  • Single-family rental (Salt Lake County, $450K purchase, 25% down): often cash-flow neutral to slightly negative at today's rates after reserves and repairs. Positive cash flow typically requires the Ogden or Davis-county markets.
  • Duplex (Ogden or Clearfield, $475K-$575K purchase, 25% down): commonly $150-$400/month positive cash flow after all operating expenses and reserves.
  • Fourplex (Ogden or Salt Lake County, $750K-$1.1M, 25% down): commonly $400-$900/month positive cash flow when managed well.
  • 5-unit or larger (commercial underwriting): cash flow depends on the specific cap rate and operator margin; 5-7% cap rates are typical in 2026 Wasatch Front for stabilized small commercial multifamily.

Why: multifamily shares overhead across units. One roof, one furnace (sometimes), one management decision across 4 leases. The marginal unit is cheaper to add than a whole second single-family house.

Appreciation: single-family wins on Wasatch Front comps

Single-family appreciation on the Wasatch Front has historically run strong because Utah is one of the fastest-growing states in the country by population and household formation. A well-chosen SFR in Salt Lake County or Utah County has reliably appreciated at or above the long-run national average over any 10-year window since the 1990s.

Multifamily (5+ unit) appreciates differently — it is valued as a business, not as a residence. If you raise rents from $1,100 to $1,350 per unit across a 10-unit building and keep expenses flat, and the local cap rate is 6%, you've added roughly $500,000 of value to the asset. That is "forced appreciation," and it is the main reason experienced Utah investors graduate from 2-4 unit multifamily to 5+ unit over time.

The 2-4 unit category is a hybrid: it finances like residential (1-4 units can use conventional mortgages) but trades like commercial in high-cap-rate markets. Appraisers blend comp-based and income-based approaches on duplexes and fourplexes.

Financing: the hard line sits between 4 and 5 units

Utah lenders draw a hard line at 5 units. Below it, the property is residential; above it, commercial. Practical implications:

  • 1 unit (SFR, condo, townhome): conventional 30-year financing, 15-25% down for non-owner-occupied, best rates on the stack.
  • 2-4 unit: conventional 30-year financing, 15-25% down, rates roughly 0.25-0.5 points above SFR. Fannie Mae conforming limits apply.
  • 5+ unit: commercial loans (25-30% down, 20-25 year amortization, 5-10 year balloon) or DSCR loans (30-year amortization but qualifies against property cash flow rather than your W-2). Rates typically 1-2 points above conventional, depending on product and sponsor.

DSCR loans are available on 1-4 unit properties too and are the common choice for investors who have multiple mortgages and want to stop qualifying personally. Minimum debt-service-coverage ratio is typically 1.0-1.2; if your rent covers your PITI plus 20%, you qualify.

Management: multifamily is operationally heavier

A single-family rental in Herriman or Clearfield with a stable family tenant can run with 3-5 hours of landlord attention a month. A 4-unit building in Ogden, even well-chosen, is often 8-15 hours a month for an owner-operator — lease rotations, between-unit issues, utility reconciliations if master-metered, trash coordination.

5+ unit buildings almost always require a property-management company (8-10% of gross rents in 2026 Utah) because the operational load exceeds what an owner can sustainably carry. Include that management fee in your underwriting from day one.

If you are time-constrained or new to being a landlord, SFR or a well-screened duplex is the right place to start. If you want to run real estate like a business, 5+ unit with professional property management is the scalable path.

Exit strategy: SFR sells faster, MFR sells on numbers

Single-family exits sell to owner-occupants, who outnumber investors 20-to-1 in any given Wasatch Front micromarket. That broad buyer pool means a well-maintained SFR typically sells inside 30-60 days when priced correctly.

Multifamily exits sell to other investors, and buyer-pool depth drops dramatically. A Salt Lake County fourplex can take 60-120 days on market in a normal cycle; a 12-unit building in Ogden may take 90-180. The payoff: multifamily transactions can be structured with 1031 exchanges into larger assets, financing can be assumable, and sophisticated buyer pools are less emotional than owner-occupant buyers. See our 1031 exchange page for the trade-up structure.

Which should you buy first in Utah?

A decision tree:

  • First-ever investment, $100K-$150K down: single-family in Ogden, Clearfield, or Roy. Learn property management on one unit with a long-term tenant pool before adding complexity.
  • Second or third investment, $200K-$350K down, Utah-based: duplex or fourplex in Ogden, Clearfield, or Salt Lake County. Capture the multifamily cash-flow premium while still using residential conventional financing.
  • $500K+ equity available, Utah or 1031-rolling from a lower-margin asset: 5-15 unit small commercial multifamily. This is where force appreciation becomes a meaningful lever and the cap-rate math starts to compound.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it better to invest in multifamily or single family in Utah?

Neither is universally better. Multifamily usually cash-flows harder per dollar invested, and 5+ unit multifamily lets you force appreciation by raising NOI. Single-family typically appreciates faster on resale comps in the Wasatch Front's fast-growing counties and is operationally lighter. Most Utah investors start with a single-family rental in Ogden, Clearfield, or Roy, then graduate to 2-4 unit multifamily on their second or third property.

How many units count as multifamily in Utah?

Any property with two or more residential units is multifamily. Utah lenders and underwriters split the category at 4 vs 5 units: 1-4 units finance as residential with conventional 30-year mortgages, while 5+ units finance as commercial multifamily with different underwriting, down payment, and amortization requirements.

Can you get a conventional loan on a multifamily property in Utah?

Yes, on 2-4 unit properties. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac conventional loans are available on duplex, triplex, and fourplex properties in Utah with 15% down for owner-occupant and 25% down for investment, subject to the FHFA conforming loan limit. Properties with 5 or more units must use a commercial loan or a DSCR loan.

What is a DSCR loan?

A DSCR (debt-service-coverage ratio) loan qualifies against the property's rental income rather than your personal W-2 or tax returns. If the property's rent covers its principal, interest, taxes, and insurance plus a typical 10-20% buffer (a DSCR of 1.1-1.2), you qualify. DSCR loans are available on both 1-4 unit and 5+ unit Utah investment properties and are the common choice for investors with multiple existing mortgages.

Which cash flows better in Utah: duplex or single family rental?

A duplex in the same Wasatch Front market and price range typically cash-flows better than a single-family rental in 2026 because a duplex shares overhead — one roof, one lot, one HOA line — across two rent streams. On a $500K Ogden duplex versus a $450K Ogden SFR with similar down payments, duplex cash flow typically exceeds SFR cash flow by $150-$300 per month after full operating expenses and reserves.
