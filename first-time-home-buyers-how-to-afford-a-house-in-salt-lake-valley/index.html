First-Time Home Buyers: How to Afford a House in Salt Lake Valley

First-Time Buyers House Hacking Investing

The honest question most first-time buyers ask us is not "what will I love." It is: can I even afford a house in Salt Lake Valley right now? Between 2021 and 2024 Salt Lake County home prices ran well ahead of wages, and even with a flatter 2026 market, the affordability math is tight. The good news: Utah Housing Corporation DPA, FHA, and the current lender willingness to pay down rates have brought the real barrier back within reach for most dual-income Wasatch Front households and many single-income ones. This guide shows the exact numbers.

If you are earlier in the journey, start with our first-time Utah buyer playbook for the full process; this article drills into the math specifically for Salt Lake County.

What it actually costs to afford a house in Salt Lake Valley

A realistic starter home in the Salt Lake Valley — Salt Lake City, Sandy, West Jordan, South Jordan, Murray, Midvale, Taylorsville, Herriman, Draper, Riverton — sits in the mid-$400Ks to high-$500Ks in 2026, per Salt Lake Board of Realtors MLS activity. Townhomes and condos trim that by $75K-$125K and are often the right call for a first purchase.

Rate assumption: the figures in this section use a mid-6% mortgage rate for illustration. Late-2026 Freddie Mac PMMS has traded in the low-to-mid 7% band, so your actual required income and monthly payment will run roughly 5–8% higher than the illustrative numbers below if you're shopping at today's rates. Lock the rate and run the math at your actual quote.

Monthly carrying cost (PITI — principal, interest, taxes, insurance) on a $450,000 home with 5% down and a mid-6% rate works out roughly as:

  • Principal & interest: ~$2,750-$2,850/month
  • Property tax (Salt Lake County residential, with primary-residence exemption): ~$250-$300/month
  • Homeowners insurance: ~$90-$120/month
  • PMI (5% down, conventional): ~$150-$225/month

That is a total PITI of roughly $3,250-$3,500 a month. If you are shopping townhomes in the $325K-$375K band, shift that whole table down about $600-$750. If you are shopping Sandy or Draper detached in the $600K+ band, add about $1,000.

The DTI math every Utah lender runs

Debt-to-income ratio is the single number that decides whether you qualify. Lenders divide your total monthly debt obligations (PITI on the new mortgage + car loans + student loans + credit card minimums + child support, etc.) by your gross monthly income.

  • Conventional loans: up to a 45-50% back-end DTI with strong credit and reserves.
  • FHA loans: up to 56.9% back-end DTI with compensating factors, though most underwriters stop at 50%.
  • UHC loans: follow the underlying first-mortgage product's DTI ceiling.

Using the $3,350 PITI from above and a 43% DTI target, the gross monthly income you need is about $7,800, or $93,600 a year — and that assumes you have no car payment, no student loan payment, and no other reported monthly debt. Every $300 of car payment shaves roughly $8,000 off your qualifying price. This is why most of our Salt Lake Valley clients pay off a car or hold off on a new lease six months before applying.

The down-payment barrier is smaller than you think

The real stall point for Salt Lake Valley first-time buyers is not the monthly payment — it is coming up with the down payment and closing costs at once. Here are the numbers on a $450,000 purchase:

  • 3% down (conventional HomeReady/Home Possible): $13,500 down + ~$11,000 closing costs = ~$24,500 needed at closing.
  • 3.5% down (FHA): $15,750 down + ~$10,000 closing costs = ~$25,750.
  • 5% down (conventional): $22,500 down + ~$11,000 closing costs = ~$33,500.
  • 20% down (conventional, no PMI): $90,000 down + ~$9,000 closing costs = ~$99,000.

The gap between the 3% path and the 20% path on the same house is roughly $75,000. For most first-time buyers, that gap is more than a year of aggressive saving, so the right move is to take the low-down-payment path, accept PMI temporarily, and request it be dropped when your loan-to-value hits 80%.

Stacking UHC DPA with FHA for a zero-cash close

Utah Housing Corporation's DPA second mortgage can cover up to 100% of your down payment and closing costs. Paired with a 3.5% FHA first mortgage, it lets qualifying Salt Lake County buyers close with effectively zero cash out of pocket (earnest money gets credited back at closing). Full details on UHC FirstHome, HomeAgain, Score 580, and No MI — income caps apply and vary by county.

The trade-off: the DPA second is a fully amortized mortgage, not a grant, so you are carrying two mortgage payments. On a $450K house with FHA 3.5% down and a UHC DPA second of about $26,000 covering closing costs plus the down payment, the second-mortgage payment adds roughly $200-$250 to your monthly carry. Your qualifying income needs to cover that, but you did not have to come up with the $25K in cash.

Household income by target Salt Lake Valley price

Below are rough gross-income floors to afford a house in Salt Lake Valley at four price points in 2026, assuming 5% down, a mid-6% rate, PMI, no other monthly debt, a 43% DTI, and Salt Lake County property tax with the primary-residence exemption. Your actual approval depends on credit, assets, and your full debt picture.

  • $325,000 (SLV townhome/condo): approximately $68,000-$74,000 annual gross.
  • $425,000 (modest detached, outer SLV): approximately $87,000-$95,000.
  • $525,000 (typical SLV detached): approximately $108,000-$118,000.
  • $650,000 (Sandy/Draper detached): approximately $133,000-$145,000.

If you are inside those bands, you likely have more options than you assume. For a deeper comparison of which loan product fits each price point, see our walkthrough of primary residence loan products in the Salt Lake Valley.

Closing costs and reserves — the number most first-timers miss

Buyer closing costs in Utah run about 2-4% of purchase price. Lenders also want to see two to three months of post-closing reserves (meaning the mortgage payment on the new house, times two or three, sitting in your account). On a $450K house that is $6,500-$10,000 of reserves on top of your down payment.

In a balanced 2026 Wasatch Front market, sellers are again paying buyer closing costs through concessions more often than they did in 2022. We regularly negotiate 2-3% seller-paid closing-cost concessions on the Salt Lake Valley listings our clients write on — that can cut $9,000-$13,000 off your cash-to-close and often fund an interest-rate buydown too. Our Salt Lake City mortgage rate guide covers the buydown math.

A realistic 12-month save-and-buy timeline

For a Salt Lake Valley household earning $90,000-$110,000, here is a 12-month plan that works:

  • Months 1-3: Get your credit above 700 (or at minimum above 620 for FHA). Pay off any car loan under a year from payoff. Open no new accounts.
  • Months 4-6: Interview three UHC-approved lenders; get a conditional pre-approval. Open a dedicated down-payment savings account and automate transfers.
  • Months 7-9: Sit with a Salt Lake Valley buyer agent and tour. Learn pricing in your target micromarket. Verify your target monthly payment is comfortable, not just approvable.
  • Months 10-12: Lock a target neighborhood, write offers, negotiate closing-cost credits, close.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much income do you need to buy a house in Salt Lake City?

For a median Salt Lake Valley starter home around $450,000 at 5% down with a mid-6% mortgage rate, you need roughly $93,000-$100,000 in annual gross household income to qualify at a 43% DTI, assuming no car payment or student loan payment. Townhomes and condos in the $325,000 range require closer to $68,000-$74,000.

What salary do you need for a $500,000 house in Utah?

At 5% down and a mid-6% rate, a $500,000 house in Utah requires roughly $103,000-$113,000 in annual gross income at a 43% back-end DTI, again assuming no other monthly debt. Adding a $400 car payment pushes that requirement up another $11,000-$12,000.

Is $100,000 a year enough to buy a house in Utah?

Yes, for most of the Salt Lake Valley a $100,000 household income qualifies you comfortably in the $425,000-$475,000 price band, which covers a wide range of townhomes, condos, and modest single-family homes in Herriman, Riverton, West Jordan, Midvale, and Taylorsville. In Sandy or Draper detached, $100,000 is tight but workable with low debt and a UHC-assisted down payment.

How much do first-time home buyers need to save in Utah?

Without DPA, plan on 5% down plus 2-4% in closing costs plus two-to-three months of reserves — roughly $30,000-$40,000 total cash for a $450,000 house. With Utah Housing Corporation DPA stacked on FHA, the out-of-pocket drops to the earnest money deposit plus the inspection and appraisal (often under $5,000) because the DPA second covers the rest.

What is the minimum down payment in Salt Lake Valley?

The practical minimum is 0% through VA loans (for eligible veterans) or through UHC DPA stacked on FHA. The next tier is 3% through a conventional HomeReady or Home Possible loan, then 3.5% through FHA. Most Salt Lake Valley first-time buyers close with 3.5% to 5% down on a primary residence.
